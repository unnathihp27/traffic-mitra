# Traffic Safety: Practical Learning Guide

> **Scope and limits:** This is original educational material for general road-safety
> awareness, with an Indian road-use context where noted. It is not an official legal
> publication, a complete statement of Indian law, or a substitute for current advice
> from the relevant transport or traffic authority. Sign designs, road rules, and
> emergency procedures can vary by location and change over time. Follow posted signs
> and current local official guidance.

## Reading signs and road markings

Traffic signs communicate instructions, warnings, and useful information. A
triangular sign commonly warns of a hazard or changing road condition ahead, such as a
sharp bend, a junction, a steep gradient, or a pedestrian crossing. The exact symbol
and local sign convention matter; slow down and read the sign rather than guessing.
Circular signs often communicate a restriction, prohibition, or requirement, while
rectangular signs often provide directions or information. Shape is a clue, not a
replacement for understanding the symbol and local rules.

Road markings help organize movement and identify boundaries. A continuous centre
line generally indicates that crossing it is restricted or unsafe under the
applicable local rules. Broken lines may permit a manoeuvre only when it is safe and
lawful. Stop lines identify where a vehicle should stop at a controlled intersection.
Zebra markings identify a pedestrian crossing area: approach cautiously, yield as
required, and never block the crossing. Lane arrows show permitted or expected
directions. Markings may be faded or obscured, so use signs, road layout, and
conditions together.

## Traffic signals and intersections

At signal-controlled junctions, obey the signal indications and any directions from
authorized traffic personnel. Stop before the stop line when required; do not enter an
intersection if traffic ahead has no room to clear it. A green indication does not
make it safe to proceed if pedestrians or other road users are still clearing the
junction. Watch for turning traffic, vehicles that may not stop, and people crossing.
Do not treat an amber indication as permission to accelerate through a junction.
Signal timing and local rules differ; use the current official rules for the place
where you are driving.

## Helmets and seat belts

Wear a properly fitted, securely fastened helmet designed for the type of two-wheeler
use. A helmet that is loose, damaged, incorrectly worn, or not fastened may not
provide its intended protection. Where a passenger is carried, the passenger also
needs appropriate protection. Replace a helmet after a significant impact and follow
the manufacturer's guidance.

Occupants should use available seat belts on every journey, including short trips.
The belt should lie snugly across the pelvis and shoulder, not across the abdomen or
neck. Children need a restraint suited to their size and installed according to its
instructions and applicable local requirements. Airbags supplement seat belts; they
do not replace them.

## Pedestrian and cyclist safety

Drivers should scan for pedestrians near crossings, bus stops, schools, markets, and
parked vehicles. Slow down where visibility is limited, avoid blocking footpaths and
crossings, and yield when required by local rules. Take particular care around
children, older people, and people with disabilities, who may need extra time to
cross.

Cyclists should be given predictable space. Check mirrors and blind spots before
turning or opening a door, and pass only when there is adequate room and visibility.
Cyclists can improve visibility with lights and reflective material in low light;
drivers should not assume a cyclist is easy to see. Everyone should communicate
intentions clearly and avoid sudden movements.

## Defensive driving and safe following

Defensive driving means anticipating hazards and leaving time and space to respond.
Look well ahead, check mirrors regularly, expect that others may make mistakes, and
keep an escape path where practical. Reduce speed near junctions, roadside activity,
parked vehicles, and places where people may enter the road. Avoid aggressive
manoeuvres and never rely on another road user's signal alone.

Leave a following gap that allows a gradual stop if the vehicle ahead brakes
suddenly. A time-based gap is more useful than a fixed distance because stopping
needs increase with speed, vehicle load, and road conditions. Choose a fixed
roadside reference: when the vehicle ahead passes it, count a consistent time before
your vehicle reaches it. Increase the gap in rain, fog, darkness, on loose or uneven
surfaces, when following a large vehicle, or when carrying a load. If another vehicle
cuts in, ease off and restore space rather than retaliating.

## Speed management

Choose a speed that allows you to stop within the clear distance you can see. Posted
limits are not a target speed in every condition. Slow down for curves, junctions,
road works, vulnerable road users, poor surfaces, congestion, and restricted
visibility. Avoid sudden acceleration and braking; smooth inputs help preserve
control. The safe speed may be lower than the posted maximum, and applicable local
limits and rules must always be followed.

## Distraction and impairment

Do not handle or look at a phone while driving. Set navigation and audio before
moving, and pull over in a safe, lawful place before attending to a device. Eating,
reaching for objects, adjusting controls, and intense conversation can also divert
attention. If tired, stop somewhere safe and rest rather than trying to push through.

Never drive when impaired by alcohol, drugs, medication that affects alertness, or
extreme fatigue. Arrange a sober, alert driver or another safe way to travel.
Impairment laws and medication effects vary; do not use this guide to determine legal
limits or whether a particular medicine is safe for driving.

## Accident-scene safety and emergency response

If you encounter a crash, first protect yourself and avoid creating another hazard.
Stop only where it is safe and lawful. If you can do so without risk, make the scene
more visible to approaching traffic and keep bystanders away from moving vehicles.
Contact the appropriate local emergency service and provide the location, apparent
hazards, and number of people involved. In India, emergency contact arrangements can
vary by service and location; use the locally verified emergency number.

Do not move an injured person unless there is an immediate danger such as fire or
oncoming traffic, because movement can worsen some injuries. Do not remove a
motorcyclist's helmet unless necessary to address an immediate life-threatening
problem and you are trained to do so. Follow instructions from emergency
dispatchers. Offer assistance within your training, do not give food or drink to a
seriously injured person, and do not enter a fire, fuel spill, or electrical hazard.
Preserve access for responders and share factual information with them.

## Vehicle safety checks

Before travelling, check that tyres appear properly inflated and have no obvious
damage, and that lights, indicators, mirrors, brakes, horn, and wipers function.
Confirm that the windscreen and mirrors are clear, the number plate and load are
secure, and fuel or charge is sufficient for the planned trip. Check that required
documents and safety equipment are present according to current local requirements.
Follow the vehicle manufacturer's maintenance schedule; a visual check does not
replace professional servicing. Do not drive a vehicle that appears unsafe.

## Rain, fog, and low visibility

In rain, slow down, increase the following gap, use lights as appropriate, and avoid
hard braking or abrupt steering. Standing water can conceal hazards and reduce tyre
grip. Do not enter floodwater when its depth or current is uncertain. If visibility
or control becomes unsafe, move to a safe stopping place away from traffic if
possible.

In fog or smoke, reduce speed, use lighting appropriate to the vehicle and local
rules, and avoid high beams that reflect from particles and worsen glare. Do not
stop in a live lane. Use lane markings only as one guide because they may be hard to
see. If you cannot see far enough to stop safely, leave the roadway at a safe place
and wait for conditions to improve.

## Indian road-safety awareness

Indian roads may involve mixed traffic, including pedestrians, cycles, two-wheelers,
three-wheelers, buses, and heavy vehicles. Expect differences in speed and direction
of movement; scan carefully and leave room. Keep intersections and pedestrian areas
clear, use protective equipment, and be patient around public transport and roadside
activity. Follow instructions from authorized personnel and current signs. For
legal obligations, penalties, licensing, or current emergency contacts, consult
official Indian and local government sources; this general guide intentionally does
not state specific fines or claim to reproduce current law.
